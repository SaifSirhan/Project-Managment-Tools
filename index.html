01 — WHAT IS A PROJECT

A project is an endeavor to accomplish a specific objective through a unique set of interrelated activities and the effective utilization of resources.

Attributes

  • Clear objective
  • Series of interdependent activities
  • Various resources
  • Specific time frame
  • Unique, one-time endeavor
  • Sponsor or customer
  • Degree of uncertainty

02 — PROJECT OBJECTIVE

A clear statement of what is to be accomplished, agreed upon by the customer and the project team.

Defined in terms of

  • End product or deliverable
  • Schedule
  • Budget

03 — PROJECT CONSTRAINTS

Successful accomplishment of the project objective is constrained by these eight factors:

ScopeWhat gets done
QualityHow well
ScheduleWhen
BudgetAt what cost
ResourcesWith what
RisksAgainst what uncertainty
Customer SatisfactionTo whose standard
Stakeholder SupportWith whose backing

04 — PROJECT LIFE CYCLE

01 Initiating Identify need, problem, or opportunity. Develop project charter. Decide if RFP is needed.
02 Planning Build the baseline plan: scope, activities, resources, schedule, budget, risks.
03 Performing Accomplish the objective. Monitor and control progress. Manage changes with sponsor approval.
04 Closing Final payments. Evaluate staff. Post-project evaluation. Lessons learned. Archive documents.

Effort rises through initiating → planning → performing, peaks late in performing, and drops sharply through closing. Baseline plan is drawn at the planning/performing boundary; accepted deliverables at closing.

06 — PROJECT MANAGEMENT

Planning, organizing, coordinating, leading, and controlling resources to accomplish the project objective.

Two major functions

  • Establish a plan
  • Execute that plan

Three elements of executing

  • Perform the work
  • Monitor and control progress
  • Control changes

07 — STAKEHOLDERS

Anyone with a stake in the project — they want to be kept informed because of potential impact.

Who they are

  • Customer / sponsor
  • Project team (including subcontractors and suppliers)
  • Organizations or groups — supportive or adversarial

Stakeholder register fields

  • Key contact information
  • Role or specific topics of interest
  • Expectations
  • Known issues
  • Areas of potential influence
  • Issue log

09 — BENEFITS

The ultimate benefit of implementing project management techniques is having a satisfied customer — whether you are the customer of your own project or a business being paid by a customer to perform a project.

Planning and communication are the two activities that most reliably prevent problems from occurring or minimize their impact when they do.

01 — PROJECT IDENTIFICATION & SELECTION

Projects start with recognizing a need, problem, or opportunity. Because organizations usually have more needs than resources, they must use a decision-making process to prioritize and select the project with the greatest overall benefit.

Selection Process

  • Evaluate needs, costs, and benefits
  • Develop criteria
  • List assumptions
  • Gather data
  • Evaluate each opportunity
  • Combine "gut" feelings with quantitative information

03 — REQUEST FOR PROPOSAL (RFP)

Used when an organization decides to outsource a project or major portion of it to an external contractor. It comprehensively describes the project requirements so contractors can submit a thorough proposal.

Guidelines for Developing an RFP

  • State project objective or purpose
  • Provide a statement of work
  • Include customer requirements
  • State deliverables the customer expects
  • State acceptance criteria
  • List customer-supplied items
  • State approvals required
  • State type of contract and payment terms
  • State schedule and key milestones
  • List format and content instructions
  • Indicate due date
  • Include evaluation criteria

04 — CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTORS

  • The need must be clearly defined before preparing an RFP.
  • Project selection should be based on which project provides the greatest overall benefit compared to costs.
  • A good RFP allows contractors to understand exactly what the customer expects.
  • An RFP should provide format and content instructions so the customer can fairly compare all proposals.
  • Never provide information to only some contractors; it gives an unfair competitive advantage.

01 — BUILDING RELATIONSHIPS

Building relationships with customers and partners is the foundation for successful funding and opportunities. Trust is key, and ethics are imperative.

Pre-RFP Efforts

  • Begin developing the relationship before the RFP is prepared
  • Helps the client identify needs
  • Positions the contractor to win the contract
  • Develops a more clearly focused response

02 — CREATING A WINNING PROPOSAL

A proposal is a selling document. It must highlight the best idea to solve the problem, emphasize benefits to the customer, and address all requirements from the RFP.

Three Main Sections

Technical Understand the need, proposed approach, benefits to customer.
Management Description of major tasks, deliverables, schedule, org, related experience.
Cost Estimated costs: labor, materials, equipment, facilities, travel, overhead, reserve, fee.

03 — CONTRACTS

A contract must be signed before starting work. It establishes communication and agreements of deliverables for a certain price.

Fixed Price Price remains fixed. Low risk for customer, high risk for contractor. For well-defined projects.
Cost-Reimbursement Price is actual costs. High risk for customer, low risk for contractor. For higher-risk projects.

04 — MEASURING PROPOSAL SUCCESS

  • Win Ratio: Number won / number submitted (all weighted equally).
  • Total Dollar Value: Value won / value submitted (more weight to larger values).

Do not bid on everything. Submit to win a fair share, but only respond where you have a better than average chance to win.

01 — PROJECT OBJECTIVE

The planning process is based on the project objective. It establishes what is to be accomplished and is often stated in the project charter or RFP.

Must include: Expected benefits, Primary end product/deliverable, Date required, Budget.

02 — PROJECT SCOPE

Defines what needs to be done and establishes a common understanding with stakeholders.

The Scope Document contains: Customer requirements, Statement of Work, Deliverables, Acceptance Criteria, and the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS).

03 — QUALITY PLAN

Complete work according to specifications and standards. The key is to do work right the first time rather than rework.

Monitor quality as the project progresses. Include references for specifications and written procedures for quality tools.

04 — WORK BREAKDOWN STRUCTURE (WBS)

A deliverable-oriented hierarchical decomposition of the project work.

Purpose: Organize project work, subdivide into manageable components, assign resources and durations, and allow the project manager to monitor progress.

Format: Graphic chart or indentured list.

05 — RESPONSIBILITY ASSIGNMENT

Designates responsible individuals for each work item (P = Primary, S = Support).

Rule: Only ONE primary responsibility per work item.

06 — SEQUENCING ACTIVITIES

A network diagram defines the sequence of activities and relationships (dependencies).

Activities are represented by boxes, consume time, and are described by a verb. Complete activities in a serial sequence or concurrently.

07 — NETWORK DIAGRAM PRINCIPLES

  • Loops: Illogical relationships that repeat forever. Not acceptable.
  • Serial: One work, two wait. (Slow).
  • Concurrent: All work at the same time (Needs triple resources).
  • Laddering: Overlapping activities for the shortest possible completion time.

01 — ESTIMATING DURATIONS

Duration must be the total elapsed time: time for the work to be done plus any associated waiting time.

Estimates should be aggressive yet realistic. The person responsible for the work should estimate the duration.

02 — EARLIEST & LATEST TIMES

Schedule calculations determine when work can start and when it must finish.

  • ES (Earliest Start): Earliest time an activity can begin based on predecessors.
  • EF (Earliest Finish): ES + Duration.
  • LS (Latest Start): LF - Duration.
  • LF (Latest Finish): Latest time an activity can finish without delaying the project.

03 — TOTAL SLACK

The difference between the earliest finish time and the required completion time.

Positive Slack: Maximum time an activity can be delayed without jeopardizing on-time completion.

Negative Slack: Amount of time an activity must be accelerated to meet the deadline.

04 — CRITICAL PATH

The longest path through the network diagram. It has the least amount of slack (usually zero).

Any delay on the critical path delays the entire project. It is the highest priority path for the project manager.

05 — PROJECT CONTROL

Regularly gather data on actual performance, compare it to the baseline plan, and take corrective action.

Address paths with negative or deteriorating slack aggressively and immediately.

01 — RESOURCE CONSTRAINTS

Available resources limit what can be done concurrently. Quantities of people, equipment, and materials may be fixed.

02 — RESOURCE LEVELING

A method to minimize fluctuations in resource requirements without extending the project schedule.

Delay noncritical activities (using their positive slack) to keep resource usage as uniform as possible.

03 — RESOURCE-LIMITED SCHEDULING

Developing the shortest schedule when the quantity of available resources is fixed and cannot be increased.

Will extend the project completion time if necessary. Priority is given to activities with the least slack.

01 — IDENTIFYING RISKS

Risk is an uncertain event that, if it occurs, can jeopardize accomplishing the project objective.

Brainstorm sources of risk. Categorize them and evaluate them based on historical information.

02 — RISK ASSESSMENT

Determine the likelihood the risk event will occur and evaluate the degree of impact on the project.

Prioritize risks based on their likelihood and impact.

03 — RISK RESPONSE PLANNING

A set of actions to prevent or reduce the likelihood or impact of a risk.

  • Avoid: Eliminate the risk entirely.
  • Mitigate: Reduce the probability or impact.
  • Accept: Acknowledge the risk and plan for a contingency if it happens.

04 — MONITORING RISKS

Regularly review and evaluate all risks throughout the project.

Identify if any new risks have appeared or if the likelihood/impact of existing risks has changed.
